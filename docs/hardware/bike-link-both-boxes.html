Both boxes: what to buy, where, and how it connects

One bike box under the seat of a Triumph that still has the regular 16-pin socket. One window box at 99 River Street, on the shop Wi-Fi. Build the bike box on the bench first. The window box can be soldered the same day. The second radio stays in the bag until the fuse current is under 50 µA.

Buy links were checked 2 November 2026. Shelf counts move. Prices below are the USD amounts those pages showed that day, before Canadian tax and shipping. This sheet is not a quote and not a radio certification.

Stop if any of these are true. The socket under the seat is the small red 6-pin. The bike is a 2006–2012 triple (K-line on pin 7 of this same plastic). You were about to power the box from pin 16. Current at the fuse is over 50 µA. A 120 Ω resistor is still between CAN H and CAN L. The radio is still at the factory 22 dBm. Customer texts stay on Wix Inbox. This build does not send them.

3. How the bike box connects

Power is the fused battery lead. The 16-pin plug does not power the box. USB is for programming on the bench. Unplug USB before the sleep test and before the box goes on the bike. Do not feed 12 V into the 5 V pin or the 3V3 pin. 3V3 is an output.

4. The 16-pin plug

Male SAE J1962 plug into the Triumph’s female socket, usually under the seat. Look into the bike’s socket, wide edge up. Top row is pins 1–8 left to right. Bottom row is pins 9–16 left to right. Pin 16 is the bottom-right corner. Buzz every pigtail wire to its pin before you trust it. Battery negative disconnected for that check.

5. How the window box connects

Wall power. No fuse and no sleep budget. Antenna side of the box toward the bikes, in a window at 99 River Street that faces where they actually park. Not a metal electrical room and not a closed cabinet. The RYLR998 has a built-in antenna and no external jack, so the whole box has to be in the window.

Board URL: https://espressif.github.io/arduino-esp32/package_esp32_index.json. Board: ESP32S3 Dev Module. Open firmware/mailbox/mailbox.ino. Copy secrets.example.h to secrets.h in that same folder. Fill in the shop Wi-Fi, the origin https://service.torontomoto.com with no path, and the ingest secret. secrets.h stays off git. Leave SOURCE as shop_bay and PLACE_NAME as 99 River Street.

USB serial prints every POST body. Until BIKE_LINK_INGEST_SECRET is set on the server, type that body into Record a reading on the motorcycle page. HTTP 401 prints once and is not retried. The route is POST /api/bike-link/readings.

6. Order of work, and the pass marks

  1. Regulator on the bench, through the fuse. No bike. No XIAO. About 3.7 V. Then the battery pads, flash, unplug USB.
  2. CAN section. Switch off when D3 is idle. Terminator absent. Measure that it is absent.
  3. Whole box from the bench supply, key-sense grounded. 3.3 V on the XIAO 3V3 pin. Asleep, current in series with the fuse under 50 µA. Over that, stop.
  4. Bench supply at 12.6 V. The voltage in the Bluetooth JSON matches the meter within 0.1 V.
  5. Battery negative off. Fuse at the post. Black wire to battery negative. CAN H and CAN L still open. Reconnect. The bike starts and idles. If it does not, pull the fuse.
  6. Key off, asleep, fuse current still under 50 µA.
  7. Meter pin 16, key off and key on. Meter pin 4 and pin 5 to battery negative.
  8. Battery off again. Connect pin 6, pin 14, and the ground pin that actually reaches battery negative. Start a second time. Resistance between pins 6 and 14 unchanged with the sleeping box plugged in.
  9. Stick TM-BL- and four digits on the box. On that motorcycle’s page, save the same sticker. Kilometers in the JSON stay blank until a Triumph in the bay answers a real request. Voltage and fault codes are the first reading.
  10. Window box on the sill. Second radio onto the bike only after both fuse measurements passed: 3.3 V, D6 to radio RX, D7 to radio TX, radio.h containing #define BIKE_LINK_RADIO 1, then reflash. Write the network id and the addresses on the mailbox.
