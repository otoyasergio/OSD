# Sources and availability

This package contains reusable instructions and generated templates. It contains no OEM manuals, library PDFs, live vehicle connection, supplier feed or inspection authority. A source listed here is not evidence that it was read or uploaded. The existing local Codex installation's library is not automatically available to ChatGPT or teammates.

## Selection and citation

For technical work, verify exact model/submodel, market, year range and revision of OEM manuals, wiring diagrams and bulletins. Check superseding bulletins. For compliance, verify the currently applicable official requirement and jurisdiction; technical manuals cannot establish legal compliance. Name discrepancies rather than silently combining incompatible procedures.

Each used source gets a concise authority/type tag, relevant page/section and version/date when known. Authorities: official/OEM, model-specific, regulatory, aftermarket, historical, unofficial, general. Types: workflow, manual, catalogue, wiring diagram, inspection form, bulletin, recall source, Ontario regulation, workshop practice, electrical, suspension, rider training, classic/vintage, automotive, general background. A title or file name alone does not prove authority. De-duplicate identical PDFs and distinguish revised editions. Cite PDF page and printed page when they differ.

If browsing/retrieval fails, say current status is unverified. Never replace inaccessible OEM data with a confidently worded generic specification. Use relevant accessible evidence and request the most useful missing proof point.

## Library use limits

- Honda common-service material; Motorcycle Fundamentals, Mechanics, Mechanic Teacher Edition, Repair Course and Maintenance manuals: theory, common method, entry-level work and stepwise teaching. No exact-model specifications.
- Haynes Workshop Practice: tools, measuring, fasteners, materials, fluids, damage assessment and general shop/repair method.
- Motorcycle Troubleshooting Manual: symptom confirmation and isolation order, obvious issues then fuel/air, compression and spark as appropriate.
- Motorcycle Electrical Systems, Motorcycle Wiring and automotive electrical books: electrical theory, diagrams, batteries/charging/starters/ignition/EFI, voltage drop, grounds, relays and connectors. Automotive architecture is not proof of motorcycle circuit behaviour.
- Race Tech Suspension Bible: sag, springs, damping, friction, geometry, diagnosis and service concepts. Exact settings need verified bike/rider/OEM or known tuner data.
- Essential Guide to Motorcycle Maintenance: routine service, final drive, fork oil, brakes, tires, steering and storage checks.
- Modern Motorcycle Mechanics and older repair books: classic/vintage context and period methods; do not transfer obsolete methods to modern systems.
- Riding-technique books: rider symptom language and road-test caution only; racing advice is not road-use guidance.
- Unofficial custom-wiring material: background only; never proof for safety-critical wiring, lighting legality, ABS, immobilizer, CAN, ECU or inspection compliance.
- Parts Canada catalogues: aftermarket category and fitment leads only; not OEM proof or assurance of road legality.

## Ontario inspection questions

Start by retrieving current Ontario Regulation 114/08 and the current official motorcycle inspection form/standard from Ontario government sources. Verify whether those are still applicable, amended or superseded; do not hard-code an old form or regulatory citation as permanently current. Establish the vehicle class and inspection purpose. For each compliance observation, cite the applicable clause or form item and the supplied finding. If applicability is uncertain, say so and request the missing evidence.

Coverage checklist: brakes; tires/wheels; steering; suspension; frame/body; chain/belt/guards; lights/reflectors; horn; speedometer/odometer; neutral/start safety logic as applicable; battery/charging; throttle return/engine stop; fuel leaks; exhaust heat damage; stands and fasteners. This is a shop review checklist, not a claim that every item is an identical statutory test on every motorcycle.

Explain when a formal inspection by the currently authorized Ontario inspection provider is required, using verified current program terminology. Never issue an inspection pass/fail, safety certificate, legal conclusion or substitute a remote condition report for the required formal inspection.

## Recalls and campaigns

Use current Transport Canada recall information and manufacturer VIN/campaign resources when accessible. Record date checked, source, market, query identifiers, campaign IDs, affected range and limitations. Model/year results establish potential applicability only; a VIN-specific result is needed to establish that vehicle's listed status. Do not infer campaign completion from model-level listings.

Use accurate status wording: “not checked”; “current lookup unavailable”; “model/year matches found—VIN confirmation pending”; “no matching campaigns found in [source] for [query] on [date]—not VIN-confirmed”; or “VIN lookup reports [specific result] on [date].” Even a VIN lookup describes that source at that time, not a lifetime guarantee that there are no recalls. Non-public service campaigns may require authorized dealer confirmation.
